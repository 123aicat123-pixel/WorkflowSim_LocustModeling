# Cloud-workflow scheduling novelty and baseline guardrail — 2026-11-04

This is a **comparative literature memo**, not a claim of exhaustive state-of-the-art review. It was assembled **after** the v19 algorithm and its first outcome-free preregistration were frozen and must not be used to retune observed v19 simulator results. Distinguish the aims and resources of papers from our static WorkflowSim makespan+cost benchmark; they need not be directly reproducible on the same platform.

## Verified prior technical work
1. Tomás Zák, Yannik Zeiträg, Rui Neves and José Rui Figueira, **“A cooperative coevolutionary genetic programming hyper-heuristic for multi-objective makespan and cost optimization in cloud workflow scheduling,”** *Computers & Operations Research*, vol.172, article 106805, December 2024. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cor.2024.106805 . The published abstract explicitly states **simultaneously coevolving task scheduling (ready-job priority) and VM selection rules** with makespan and cost objectives, and uses NSGA-II for the multiobjective search. This is direct novelty-counterevidence to the simplistic claim “first to optimize task ordering and VM mapping jointly.” Their reported improvement is in their own scenarios and cannot be imported into WorkflowSim as if reproduced.
2. Aanchal Agrawal and Arun Kumar Pal, **“Cluster and reinforcement learning-based multi-objective evolutionary algorithm for joint scheduling of virtual machines and prioritize tasks in cloud computing,”** *Swarm and Evolutionary Computation*, volume 99, article 102156, Dec. 2025, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.swevo.2025.102156 . Their source abstract identifies **three-tier encoding, RL-guided local search, preselection, dynamic allocation**, optimizing makespan, cost and tardiness in joint VM/task decisions. Methodologically adjacent, not assumed to be an identical DAG-specific simulator baseline.
3. **“A Reinforcement Learning-Based Population Hyper-Heuristic for Energy-Efficient Cloud Workflow Scheduling Problem,”** *IEEE Transactions on Services Computing* 18(5), pp.2545–2558, 2025. DOI https://doi.org/10.1109/TSC.2025.3589126 . Uses six low-level heuristics with Q-learning to select them and local search. Relevant as high-level heuristic-selection precedent; its objective emphasis includes energy and must not be silently treated as a makespan+cost apples-to-apples baseline.
4. **“Multi-Tree Genetic Programming Hyper-Heuristic for Dynamic Flexible Workflow Scheduling in Multi-Clouds,”** *IEEE Transactions on Services Computing*, 17(5), 2024, pp.2687–2703, DOI https://doi.org/10.1109/TSC.2024.3394691 . Evolves cloud/resource/task selection rules in a dynamic multi-cloud simulator for cost/deadline reliability; distinct system assumptions, relevant to policy evolution novelty and generalization.
5. **“Cloud-native Workflow Scheduling using a Hybrid Priority Rule, Dynamic Resource Allocation, and Dynamic Task Partition,”** SoCC 2024, IBM Research page https://research.ibm.com/publications/cloud-native-workflow-scheduling-using-a-hybrid-priority-rule-dynamic-resource-allocation-and-dynamic-task-partition . Shows combining child count with weighted shortest critical path priority in modern workflow scheduling; static upward rank and shortest task rules are not new by themselves.

## What our platform has actually established
- Independent **source-of-truth simulator-vs-planner** reconciliation, source seed/mapping identity, compulsory original Task-level traces, no hidden simulator data in FLA fitness decisions.
- On known DAG instances, alternate fixed VM-ready ordering can materially HELP or HARM true makespan while VM assignment remains unchanged (e.g. Sipht opposite ±1.36%/+10.65% under UPWARD and different fixed mapping).
- Event-driven priority-aware ready estimator forecasts large priority-sensitive real makespan shifts in separate DAX *instances*, but does not always predict correct directions (CyberShake SJF counterexample).
- Equal genotype call counts / K16 simulator replay budgets and one cross-arm actual HV reference; initial three-policy portfolio underperformed FLA baseline on most blocks; **restart-matched test** found FLA priority islands −0.000228 mean HV vs three FIFO restarts, 1 win/3 losses. NSGA-II full SJF showed stronger 4/4 limited benefits than FLA full SJF in the tested blocks.
- New FLA-v19 risk-bounded VM-ready contention proposal is exploratory; do not confuse its activation count with effectiveness. The primary endpoint is preregistered equal-K16 **actual simulator HV** against original v12/v18 and seeded NSGA-II, with explicit CPU overhead and group-specific Paretoguards.

## Publication novelty gate
A claim worth peer review must offer more than “FLA plus an old ready-job priority rule”:
1. A clearly specified **conflict-aware risk mechanism** or a Pareto-aware planner that materially differs from published rule-learning and hyper-heuristic selection;
2. independent fairness audits and **time-matched** comparator rather than only equal counted genotypes when the proposed method does hidden extra model forecasts;
3. **untouched workflow-family** test cases (new unviewed DAX families/data, not merely new sizes of previously used families) with multiple VM heterogeneity regimes and at least 20 independent randomized trials;
4. true Makespan/Cost Pareto HV and IGD+ with shared references, cost/energy extensions only if definitions and simulator event truth support them, all failures and limitations disclosed;
5. same compound priority/VM representation for all metaheuristic baselines or a clear separation of the value of new representation from FLA-specific search operator, plus ablation of priority-only, VM-only, risk-gate, readiness model, and selection mechanisms.

**Do not claim that adding a priority dimension or an RL bandit is itself novel: multiple peer-reviewed publications already do that.** No source-master modifications or default algorithm changes are justified by this memo.
